Customer psychology

Sell differently to different travellers

A destination is not “good” in isolation. It is good only when it fits the customer’s purpose, pace, budget, risk tolerance and expectations.

💑

Honeymoon Couple

Emotion + privacy

Prioritise romance, photos, privacy, unique stays and low-friction transfers. Sell moments, not a checklist.

👨‍👩‍👧

Family with Kids

Safety + convenience

Check child policies, meal options, activity age limits, transfer time, rest breaks and nearby medical support.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Friends Group

Energy + value

Build around activities, nightlife, social experiences, group transfers and transparent cost sharing.

🧓

Senior Travellers

Pace + comfort

Avoid rushed routing, steep walking, high altitude without checks, long transfers and uncertain meal arrangements.

🎒

Budget Explorer

Maximum experience

Use shoulder season, shared transfers, central value hotels and must-do activities. Explain trade-offs honestly.

💎

Luxury Traveller

Exclusivity + control

Lead with location, private transfers, premium access, concierge support, room category and flexibility.

🌐

First-Time International

Assurance + clarity

Simplify visa, currency, airport flow, documentation, food availability, local laws and on-trip support.

🧗

Adventure Traveller

Challenge + safety

Qualify fitness, altitude tolerance, age/weight limits, weather dependency, insurance and backup plans.

Consultative sales lab

From enquiry to ethical closure

The best travel seller does not push a destination. They reduce uncertainty, connect desire to a feasible plan and help the customer decide.

Introduce yourself, confirm available time, acknowledge the enquiry source and set the agenda: “I’ll understand the trip first, then suggest the two best-fit options with a clear cost comparison.”
Ask: who is travelling, purpose, dates and flexibility, departure city, budget range, previous travel, pace, hotel expectation, food, passports/visa, health constraints and non-negotiable experiences.
Give no more than two or three options. Explain fit using the customer’s own words: season, budget, duration, traveller profile and desired experience. Also explain what you intentionally did not recommend.
Compare total value: hotel location, transfer model, ticket slots, meals, support, flexibility and hidden cost exposure. Avoid reading the itinerary line by line without context.
Use the five-step model below. Probe the real issue before offering a discount. “Expensive” may mean trust gap, unclear value, payment pressure, competitor anchoring or wrong product fit.
“Your package is expensive.”“Absolutely, price matters. May I check whether the difference is in the total package value or just the headline price? Let us compare the hotel location, transfers, included tickets, meals and support side by side.”
AcknowledgeProbeClarifyReframeClose
“Send details on WhatsApp; I’ll check.”“I’ll send a clean comparison. Before I do, which matters more: keeping the trip under budget or including the signature experiences? That will help me avoid sending an irrelevant package.”
“I can book flights and hotels myself.”“You certainly can. The value of a package is in connected planning—correct routing, confirmed activity slots, transfers, support and one-point accountability. We can also keep flights independent and package only the ground experience.”
Offer design choices before discount: alternate dates, hotel category, shared transfer, activity prioritisation, room configuration or payment plan. Record every approved deviation.
Use choice closes, summary closes and next-step closes. Fix a specific follow-up time. Update CRM with decision-maker, concern, budget, promised revision and next action.

MakeMyTrip Battlecard

OTA ecosystem
Strong at

Inventory breadth, bundled components, visible offers, EMI and quick digital booking.

Do not compete only on

Coupon, strike-through price or app convenience.

Win with

A line-by-line value sheet: location, transfer mode, activity timing, free time, support and cancellation exposure.

Trainer drill

Give two packages with a ₹12,000 gap and ask the fresher to explain the gap without saying “ours is better”.

Pickyourtrail Battlecard

Custom holiday tech
Strong at

International customisation, flexible itineraries, modular planning and concierge positioning.

Customer benchmark

Fast iterations and visibility into hotels, transfers and experiences.

Win with

Sharper consultation, stronger experience curation and a clear explanation of why every day exists in the itinerary.

Trainer drill

Rebuild a generic Bali enquiry into two differentiated routes in under ten minutes.

Thomas Cook Battlecard

Assisted ecosystem
Strong at

Visa facilitation, forex, insurance, group programmes and end-to-end assisted travel.

Customer benchmark

Trust, documentation confidence and institutional process.

Win with

Accurate scope, response agility, experience richness and visible service milestones before and during travel.

Trainer drill

Handle: “They are a much older brand, why should I book with you?”

Veena World Battlecard

Group-tour specialist
Strong at

Tour managers, fixed departures, air-inclusive group formats and segment-specific tours.

Customer benchmark

Guided convenience, companionship, meals and fixed structure.

Win with

Diagnose whether the traveller wants a group or private trip. Sell flexibility only when pace and privacy matter.

Trainer drill

Compare a Europe group tour versus a private itinerary for a senior couple.

DIY Battlecard

Self-booking
Strong at

Control, component-level choice and perceived savings.

Hidden risk

Disconnected timings, transfer gaps, non-refundable tickets and no single escalation owner.

Win with

Offer a hybrid: customer books flights while you manage the land package, experiences and support.

Trainer drill

Calculate the real cost of airport transfer, city tax, baggage and attraction tickets.

Local Agent Battlecard

Relationship-led
Strong at

Personal relationship, local trust and negotiation familiarity.

Customer benchmark

One known person who “will manage everything”.

Win with

Documented inclusions, supplier quality, payment safety, service SLAs and professional escalation—not negative comments.

Trainer drill

Handle: “My neighbourhood agent knows my family and is ₹8,000 cheaper.”

1. Same travel inputsDates, passengers, departure city, room occupancy and child ages.
2. Same product depthHotel name/location, meals, transfer mode and attraction ticket category.
3. Same risk termsCancellation, visa, weather, refund, tax and non-refundable components.
4. Same service promisePre-trip support, on-trip escalation, response window and accountability.

USP playbook

Turn features into customer-specific proof

Do not dump every USP. Select two or three that directly solve the customer’s concern, then support them with a visible itinerary, inclusion, policy or service commitment.

🎯

Experience-first planning

Position the trip around the moments the customer wants—not only flight and hotel components.

Proof to showNamed activities, time slots, route logic and signature experiences.
🧭

Destination depth

Public destination pages, itineraries and activity inventory can support more informed route recommendations.

Use whenThe customer is confused between two destinations or circuits.
🧩

Customisation choices

Build value, comfort and premium versions by changing stay, pace, transfers and experiences.

Proof to showTwo side-by-side options with visible trade-offs.
🎟️

Activity-rich inventory

Use experience options to differentiate an itinerary beyond generic sightseeing.

Use whenFriends, honeymooners and repeat travellers want memorable moments.
🛡️

Connected trip support

A packaged route can provide a clearer escalation path than disconnected component bookings.

Proof to showExact pre-trip and on-trip support scope; never overpromise.
👥

Multiple traveller segments

Honeymoon, family, adventure, luxury and group needs can be mapped to different routes and pacing.

Use whenThe same destination must be redesigned for a different persona.
📚

Content-backed consultation

Destination information helps an advisor explain season, duration, places and activity fit more confidently.

Proof to showRelevant public guide or package page—not vague claims.
⚖️

Value transparency

Break the package into stays, transfers, tickets, meals, support and risk terms to make comparison fair.

Use whenThe conversation is stuck only on the lowest headline price.
USP formula: “Because you said [customer priority], this matters: [relevant USP]. You can see it in [specific proof]. It reduces [customer risk] and gives you [desired outcome].”
Customer FAQs

Answer clearly, accurately and without overpromising

Use the FAQ library for training and live-call preparation. Current price, visa, airline, weather and entry rules must always be revalidated before commitment.

Trainer compliance reminder: Never guarantee visas, weather, specific wildlife sightings, Northern Lights, room upgrades, early check-in, airline schedule stability or refunds outside written policy. Capture every commitment in CRM and the approved quotation.
